Nashville's academy show was a two-sided night. The good wrestling was
there, but the card rating was * 3/4, sunk by a 31-minute four corners
match and a Leatherface squash that nobody will want to see again.

The bigger story happened before the bell. Michael Modest, who was promised
the first title shot at First Strike, no-showed, and so did Ace Steel. That
wiped out both of the matches announced last month (Modest vs. Jericho and
Bagwell vs. Steel), and Ron Bass vs. Mike Awesome didn't happen either.
Rocky Johnson and the trainers had to rebuild the card on the fly, and you
could tell.

The Lightning Kid and Judge Dread opened with a 19-minute match that was
the best thing on the show along with the main event, *** 1/2. Kid won with
a moonsault. He already looks like the most exciting worker in the
building.

Chris Benoit, in his first appearance here, beat Johnny B. Badd in ten
minutes with a Northern Lights suplex, ***. Benoit is the most polished
technician on this roster and should be treated as a top contender from day
one. Badd did his part and lost nothing.

Brad Armstrong and Bobby Duncum Jr. beat Devon Hughes and Buzz Sawyer when
Armstrong pinned Sawyer with a flying cross body, ** 3/4. With the
no-shows, Sawyer got pressed into service as Hughes' partner. Armstrong is
a veteran hand who makes everyone look good, and Duncum Jr. has room to
grow.

The four corners match went 31:35 and got **. Tatanka eliminated Lance
Storm with the End of the Trail at 19:42, Awesome pinned Bagwell with the
Awesome Bomb at 27:41, and Awesome won when Tatanka got himself
disqualified at 31:35. It was far too long for this crowd. The real damage
is that Bagwell hurt his neck and is out about 10 cards. At one show a
month, that keeps last month's title challenger off TV well into 1992.

Leatherface powerbombed Steven Dunn in 13:22 for -* 1/2. Paul Bearer
interfered against Leatherface, which raises a question nobody has answered
yet: what is Paul Bearer doing in Nashville? Dunn also hurt his neck but
should miss only one card.

Chris Jericho retained the Power Pro Heavyweight title in the main event,
making Bart Gunn pass out in the Lion Tamer at 10:02, *** 1/2. Gunn was a
fill-in challenger and looked better than expected. Jericho is carrying the
belt well in his first defense.

Where it goes from here: Modest's no-show either gets punished or turns
into a storyline, since he spent a month calling himself the next champion.
Benoit and the Lightning Kid are the obvious new challengers. Awesome and
Tatanka have unfinished business after the DQ finish, and somebody needs to
explain Paul Bearer.
