Match observations: 

Match observations:

Venue: Pontins Camber Sands
City: Camber, East Sussex
Date: Wednesday, July 10, 1991
Attendance: 1,200 (sellout)
Average ticket: $4.17
Gate: $5,000 (flat fee, show sold to the resort)

All Star Wrestling's third holiday camp show drew the usual sellout and
rated **1/4, the same as Bognor. Two matches carried it and two dragged it
down. It also had a debut, two programs set in motion and the first
appearance of the summer by the biggest star in British wrestling.

Brian Dixon opened with a short welcome and confirmed the title match. He
told the crowd Haystacks wouldn't be allowed to hide on the outside this
time, which set up the finish nicely.

Skull Murphy made his ASW debut in the opener and lost to Doc Dean, who
pinned him with the magistral cradle in 12:16. It went -***1/2, one of the
worst ratings on any card in the world this month. Four days after Dean
went 29 minutes with Backlund at *****1/2, he couldn't get anything out of
Murphy, and twelve minutes was about nine too many. It's not the debut
anyone wanted. Dean wasn't hurt by the result, but Murphy shouldn't be in a
singles match again until we know what he can do. A tag match where someone
else carries the work is the place to find out.

Gary Albright pinned Ivan Putski with a German suplex in 9:11 for **1/4. It
did its job. Albright looked like a powerhouse, Putski had the crowd behind
him, and the result sets up a rematch at Pontins Prestatyn on July 24. The
idea there is Putski getting his revenge with the Polish Hammer. Albright
is 1-1 on the tour after submitting to Haystacks at Southport, and he needs
this feud to establish what he is in this territory.

Johnny Saint pinned Steve Regal with a cradle in 12:31. At ***1/4, it was
the second-best match on the card and the best Regal has had on the tour.
The story came after the bell. Regal attacked Saint from behind as he was
celebrating, put the boots to him and left him lying, then told the crowd
Saint's style was music-hall wrestling that belongs to the past. That's the
start of a proper feud, and it's the one Regal has needed since Southport.
Saint has now been robbed twice against Haystacks and attacked by Regal,
and the camp crowds love him. He's the most sympathetic babyface on the
tour.

Bob Backlund made Fit Finlay pass out in the crossface chickenwing in
18:21. This was ****1/4, the match of the night again. That's ****3/4,
*****1/2 and ****1/4 in his three matches on the tour, which is ridiculous
for a ballroom tour. Finlay held up his end and lost nothing in defeat. The
Backlund Challenge is working exactly as intended.

The main event went 20:09 and rated *1/2. Haystacks spent most of it
leaning on Robbie Brookside, and in the closing minutes he had Brookside
trapped in the corner and was squashing the life out of him. That's when
Big Daddy made his first appearance of the summer. He came through the
crowd, through the ropes and straight at Haystacks to save Brookside, and
the ballroom came unglued. He splashed Haystacks and chased him from the
ring.

Because Big Daddy interfered on Brookside's behalf, the referee
disqualified Brookside. Haystacks keeps the title and is now 3-0 on the
tour. Brookside took his first loss. In the moment nobody in the building
cared: they'd just seen Big Daddy run Haystacks off. On paper, though, the
rescue cost Brookside his title shot. That's a better story than the one
that was drawn up, if it's handled right. Brookside can be frustrated
without turning on anyone, and Big Daddy can make it up to him by teaming
with him against Haystacks at Skegness on July 17.

The problem is the length. Twenty minutes of Haystacks is too much for
anyone, and the rating shows it. His matches need to stay under ten minutes
for the rest of the summer, and Big Daddy's need to be shorter still.

Three shows in, ASW has a workrate star in Backlund, a hot crowd act in
Saint, a champion the fans genuinely want to see lose and, as of tonight,
Big Daddy. It also has a booking problem: the bottom of the card keeps
dragging the ratings down, and both of the last two main events have rated
under **. The talent to fix that is already on the roster. It's a question
of keeping the matches short where they need to be short.
