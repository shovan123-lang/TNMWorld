Match observations: 

ASW Holiday Camp Action! - Pontins Southport Erupts with International Stars!

From the pages of All Star Wrestling Weekly, Summer 1991 Edition
Pontins Southport was packed to the rafters as All Star Wrestling rolled
into town with a night of international action that had holidaymakers buzzing
long after the final bell. Promoter Brian Dixon himself stepped into the ring 
to welcome families, campers, and die-hard grappling fans to what he promised
would be “a night you’ll be talking about all summer.” And he wasn’t wrong.

The evening delivered a rare blend of British mat mastery, American intensity,
Japanese finesse, and the raw power of some of the sport’s most imposing 
heavyweights. From technical showcases to bruising brawls, the Southport 
faithful were treated to a card that felt more like a world tour compressed
into one electric night.

The legendary Tony St. Clair opened the wrestling portion of the evening with
a masterclass in timing and technique. Facing the spectacular high-flying 
pioneer El Gran Hamada, St Clair kept the bout grounded early, forcing Hamada
to wrestle his match. Hamada dazzled with bursts of aerial offense, but
St. Clair's veteran savvy prevailed as he snatched a picture perfect German
Suplex for the victory. A gentlemen's handshake afterward drew warm applause
from the crowd. 

In one of the most unlikely pairings ever seen on a Pontis stage,
the charismatic Iceman King Parsons teamed with the immovable mountain known
as Giant Haystacks. Across the ring stood world-class grapplers: The
suplex machine Gary Albright and the legendary Dory Funk Jr. 

Albright and Funk tried to chop the giant down, but Haystacks refused to
budge. Parsons provided the flash, Haystacks the force, and the finish came
in surreal fashion: Haystacks clamped a side headlock on Albright so
crushing that the American powerhouse had no choice but to submit. The crowd
roared as the Iceman danced around the ring, celebrating the unlikeliest
tap-out in ASW history. 

Young Liverpool sensation Doc Dean continued his rise with a clever, fast
paced win over the crafty Hector Guerrero. Guerrero controlled much of
the bout with slick chain wrestling, but Deans speed proved decisive. In
a flash, he rolled Guerrero into a tight Magistral Cradle, scoring the
win that had the younger fans chanting his name. 

The match of the night. Former WWF Champion Bob Backlund and Calgary's own 
Bruce Hart delivered a grueling, technical war that had the entire hall on edge.
Hart targeted Backlund's arm with relentless precision, but Backlund's
conditioning and intensity carried him through. After nearly 24 minutes 
of pure wrestling, Backlund locked in the dreaded Crossface Chickenwing, 
and Hart—fading fast—passed out rather than submit. A standing ovation 
followed for both men.

The ever improving Robbie Brookside scored a major win over the loud, 
abrasive Canadian veteran Iron Mike Sharpe. Sharpe's bullying tactics kept
Brookide on the defensive early, but the Liverpool lad rallied with crisp
encounters and youthful fire. A beautifully executed bridging German
suplex sealed the victory and continued Brooksides steady climb up the ASW
ranks. 

In our main event, two of Britians finest collided in a rugged, bruising 
encounter. Fit Finlay, the belfast bruiser, brought his trademark intensity,
while a young Steve Regal matched him hold for hold. The bout escalated into 
a stiff, hard hitting countest that had the crowd wincing with every strike.
Finlay ultimately planted Regal with a devestating Tombstone Piledriver,
earning a decisive victory in a match that felt like a preview of future
main events.
