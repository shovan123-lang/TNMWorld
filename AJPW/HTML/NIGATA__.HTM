Match observations: 

Summer Action Series Tour | July 7th,1991
Day 4: Nigata Japan |Venue: Nigata City Gymnasium
Attendance: 2,700 AVG: Ticket $25.00 
Gate: $67,500 Broadcast: N/A

Too Cold Scorpio vs Richard Slinger
Scorpio opened the night with a crisp, energetic win, hitting the Flying
Scorpio Splash at 10:40. The match was short on heat, but long on athleticism.
Slinger bumped big, Scorpio dazzled and the crowd warmed up nicely. A
solid match to start the show. 

Terry Gordy vs Giant Kimala II
The match began with Gordy trying to impose his wrestling fundamentals early,
shooting in for a fishermans suplex. Kamala responded with a kneelift that
knocked the wind out of Gordy. The end of the match saw Gordy lock him in
a sleeperhold that Kimala was able to fend off. Gordy attempred a swinging
neckbreaker, but thats when Kimala yanked refferee Mitsu Matsune into the
line of fire. Mitsune shook it off, saw enough, and called for the bell. 
The crowd reaction remained split- some cheered Gordy's grit, others confused
by the messy finish- but the decision kept Gordy strong on the eyes of the 
fans, while Kimala II showed how unpredictble and uncontrollable of a monster
that he really is. 

Ogawa/Inoue/Tommy Rodgers vs Can-Am Express & Johnny Ace
A lively six man with good pace. Ogawa scoring the fall on Kroffat with
the Backdrop Suplex Hold was a mild surprise and a nice elevation moment.
Rodgers and Ace kept the American flavor strong, while Inoue played the
underdog well. With Ogawas pinfall, he and Inoue could be looking at a tag
match with the Can Am Express for the titles. 

Kenta Kobashi vs Billy Joe Travis
A near squash with Kobashi blasting through Travis in under two minutes.
The burning Lariat looked fantastic. 

The British Bulldogs came out to the ring and were attempting a 2 on 1 
attack on Kobashi until Masa Fuchi ran down to protect him. Kobashi
headed to the back as Kikuchi made his way into the ring to team with Fuchi,
and get a tag match started. 

The British Bulldogs vs Masa Fuchi and Tsuyoshi Kikuchi
A standout tag match, Dynamite was sharp, Davey was explosive, and Kikuchi
took a heroic beating. Dynamite's flying headbutt sealed it at 10:38.

As Kikuchi say motionless in the ring, out comes another tag team, the Malenko
brothers. They demanded the ref ring the bell after saying they had more
of a beating ready for Kikuchi. Referee Wada was bullied into starting the
match. After a few devestating power moves, Kawada came out to help his friend.
The match ended up being a technical gem and the match of the night, until
our Main Event. Kawada brought stiffness, the Malenkos brought precision, and
Kikuchi once again showed his fighting spirit, playing the roll of the valiant
crash-test dummy. Deans Texas Cloverleaf finish was decisive and clean.

Mitsuharu Misawa vs Dory Funk Jr. 
The evenings main event was a beautiful,deliberate, old-school match. Misawa
worked patiently, letting Dory dictate the early pace before taking over
with escalating elbows. The Running Elbowsmash finish at 20:27 got a strong
reaction. 

Promoters Thoughts: 
- Baba was pleased with the steady Niigata turnout. Not a blowaway house,
but solid for the mid-tour stop. The Bulldogs and Misawa remain the strongest
drawing acts on this loop. 

-The Malenkos vs Kawada/Kikuchi match was the talk of the lockeroom. This
was the type of technical, credible wrestling Baba wants anchoring the
Mid-Card. 

-Misawa's poise, opposite Dory Funk Jr. stood out to managment. Baba sees 
these as essential "education bouts" to round out Misawa's long term
Ace presentation. 

-Foreign Talent have been a highlight of the tour. The Bulldogs seem to be 
leading the pack among the for foreigners. Some backstage are concerned 
about Dynamite's physical condition long-term. Scorpio is viewed as a future
star if he can stay consistent.
